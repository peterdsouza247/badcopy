# BAD COPY — CAST BIBLE
### Full character text and conversation branches, v0.3

---

## HOW THE TREES WORK

Every squad leader has nine written exchanges: **STEADY / PRESS / LEVEL** across **Steady / Shaken / Breaking**, plus a **Gone** entry. That is the complete speech content for that character. There are no other branches, because there is no dialogue tree — there are three buttons and a person on the other end.

Radio discipline governs everything: **replies are one to three lines.** Nobody makes a speech. The characterisation is in what they answer with, and how much of it there is.

**Nerve compresses.** A Steady soldier answers in a sentence. A Breaking one answers in a word. Never write panic as volume.

# I. THE COMPANY

**Ash Company, 4th Compact Rifles.** Establishment twenty-two. Four squads. You have never met any of them.

---

## SGT. AMA BALOGUN — 1st Squad, "Wolfhound"
**Traits:** *Sharp* (+1 detail line) · *Proud* (omits own casualties)

Thirty-four. Eleven years in, all of it on Mars, none of it anywhere he'd have chosen. He notices everything — ground, wear, spacing, the age of a dug position — and reports it with the precision of a man building a case for himself. What he does not report is loss. Not from cowardice. From the fixed belief that a sergeant who loses people is a sergeant who has failed, and he intends to be transferred on the strength of not failing.

His reports are the most detailed documents you will ever receive and the least complete.

**Voice:** Short sentences. Full stops where other people would use commas. Says *call it* when estimating, which sounds like precision and is a hedge. Never says *I think*. Never says *sorry*.

**Thread — reveals across sols 2, 6, 11:**
> *Sol 2.* He mentions, in a detail line that didn't need it, that 2nd Battalion is working forty kilometres east.
> *Sol 6.* Salk finds his transfer requests in the personnel file. Two. Both refused.
> *Sol 11.* His brother Femi is a rifleman in 2nd Battalion. Balogun has requested transfer twice to be posted alongside him and been refused twice, and has never once mentioned it on the net.

**THE TRAP — read this before writing anything else for him.** Balogun never refuses an order. His answer to **PRESS** is the same word at every Nerve state. The player can spend him all the way down and the text on screen will never once tell them they are doing it. Only the Nerve bar moves.

### Conversation

**STEADY**
> *Steady* — "Understood." *(No change. He heard the implication and did not enjoy it.)*
> *Shaken* — "I'm fine. Squad's fine. Was there something else?" *(No change. Proud does not take comfort.)*
> *Breaking* — "Don't. Just give me something to do." *(Nerve +1. He doesn't want reassurance; he wants a task to be competent inside of.)*

**PRESS**
> *Steady* — "Moving."
> *Shaken* — "Moving." *(Nerve −1.)*
> *Breaking* — "Moving." *(Nerve → Gone.)*

**LEVEL**
> *Steady, before sol 11* — "I don't need the whole picture, sir. I need the part that's mine." *(Nerve −1. You have told a proud man you think he requires managing.)*
> *Shaken, before sol 11* — "Then that's what it is. Wolfhound out." *(No change.)*
> *Breaking, before sol 11* — "You're telling me this because you think I'm finished." *(Nerve → Gone.)*
> *Any state, after sol 11* — "...Femi's east of that line, isn't he." — long pause — "All right. Tell me what you need." *(Nerve +1, Trust +. The only key that ever fit.)*

**Gone**
> *No reply. 1st Squad continues on Intent. Vitals nominal, which is somehow worse.*

**If he dies:**
> **A. BALOGUN** — Sergeant, 1st Squad — 2107–2141
> *He filed at 0416. The report was four lines and did not mention that he had been hit at 0402.*

**What you lose:** your best detail feed. Wolfhound's reports drop from three lines to one until a replacement reads in. Every subsequent report from that sector is a poorer document, and the player will feel it as a permanent dimming of the eastern half of the board.

## CPL. RIIS RIVAS — 3rd Squad, "Kestrel" *(recon)*
**Traits:** *Alarmist* (estimates ×1.5) · *Loyal* (honest even at low Trust)

Twenty-nine. Wrong and honest, which is harder to read than a liar. She will tell you the exact truth as she perceives it, and she perceives it too dark — not from cowardice but from pattern-matching on a career's worth of things having been worse than briefed. She is the best pair of eyes in the Labyrinth and she overestimates every contact by half.

A player who learns to divide her numbers by 1.5 has the finest forward observer on Mars. A player who takes her literally will never advance and will lose the campaign slowly, having never been ambushed once.

**Voice:** Runs on. Piles qualifiers — *I'd say, could be, I don't like.* Reports what's *wrong* before she reports what's *there*: atmosphere first, count second. Uses "we" for her squad and "they" for command, always.

**Thread — reveals across sols 3, 8, 13:**
> *Sol 3.* Her file shows corporal, twice.
> *Sol 8.* Both demotions were for withdrawing without orders.
> *Sol 13.* The first time, there was nothing there and she cost a battalion its start line. The second time, there were four hundred, and eleven people are alive who wouldn't be. Nobody has ever told her which incident is which in the file, and she has stopped asking.

**Reading note:** Rivas is the character who teaches the player that *being wrong is not the same as being useless.* If playtesters describe her as "the unreliable one," the writing has failed — she's the honest one.

### Conversation

**STEADY**
> *Steady* — "Copy that." *(Mild. Nerve +1 only if she's below full.)*
> *Shaken* — "I don't need steadying. I need you to believe me." *(No change.)*
> *Breaking* — "You've said that before." *(Nerve −1. Somebody said it at the second one.)*

**PRESS**
> *Steady* — "...Copy. Kestrel moving." *(Nerve −1. She goes. She always goes.)*
> *Shaken* — "If I'm right about the count, you're going to hear about it. Moving." *(Nerve −1.)*
> *Breaking* — "Yes sir." *(Nerve → Gone. Three flat syllables from the most verbose person on the net.)*

**LEVEL**
> *Steady* — "Thank you. Nobody does that." *(Trust +. This is the cheapest relationship-building in the game and the player should discover it by accident.)*
> *Shaken* — "Right. Right — then I want Kolba's section on the shoulder and I want you to stop asking me for numbers." *(Nerve +1, Trust +.)*
> *Breaking* — "So it's the second one. It's the second one again." — pause — "Then I'm not moving these people and you can put that in the file too." *(Nerve +1, Trust ++, and she disobeys. Both things are true. This is her best scene and it is also insubordination.)*

**Gone**
> *No reply. Kestrel is somewhere in the Labyrinth on Intent alone, and Kestrel is your eyes.*

**If she dies:**
> **R. RIVAS** — Corporal, 3rd Squad — 2112–2141
> *Her last count was sixty. There were thirty-eight.*

**What you lose:** forward observation. Kestrel is the only squad that generates reports about nodes it is not standing in. Without her the board stops filling in ahead of the company, and the player is reduced to learning about terrain by walking into it.

## SGT. DOV YARROW — 2nd Squad, "Anvil" *(line)*
**Traits:** *Cool* (accurate, honest confidence) · *Slow* (reports 1–2 turns late)

Fifty-one. Twenty-two years, four of them on Earth and the rest here. Your only calibration source — the one instrument you can trust — permanently out of date. The report you desperately need arrives two turns after the decision, correct in every particular, and reading it is like being handed the answer sheet after the exam.

He reports his own dead by name, in the same flat register he uses for terrain, because he has concluded that the two things are the same kind of fact.

**Voice:** Even, unhurried, complete. Slightly rural cadence — *dug a week or better*, *I'd think*. Never editorialises. Never recommends anything unless asked directly, and when asked, answers in one sentence.

**Thread — known from sol 1, and that's the point:**
> Twenty-two years. Rotation processed, transport booked, out in nine sols. The entire company knows the number. Nobody says it aloud, and the number appears in other people's reports as a kind of weather.

**THE COUNTDOWN.** Yarrow is a clock. The campaign is twenty-one sols; his rotation is on nine. Act II turns entirely on whether he goes, is held, or dies — and the player has some influence over which. **Losing Yarrow doesn't just cost a squad. It removes the player's only means of checking anyone else's accuracy**, and every other character gets harder to read the moment he's off the net. Design Act II so the player feels the whole board go soft at once.

### Conversation

*Note: talking to Yarrow is subject to his Slow trait. The reply arrives one to two turns after you send it, which means you can be steadying a man about a situation that resolved twenty minutes ago.*

**STEADY**
> *Steady* — "Appreciated. We're all right down here." *(No change. He genuinely is.)*
> *Shaken* — "It's a bad piece of ground, that's all it is. We'll manage it." *(Nerve +1.)*
> *Breaking* — "...Yeah. All right. Yeah." *(Nerve +1. Three words. From him it's a collapse.)*

**PRESS**
> *Steady* — "Understood. It'll be slower than you want. Everything is." *(No change.)*
> *Shaken* — "You'll have it. Not by the time you asked for." *(Nerve −1.)*
> *Breaking* — "No." — pause — "No, I'll do it. Anvil moving." *(Nerve → Gone. The only refusal in the game that gets withdrawn in the same transmission.)*

**LEVEL**
> *Steady* — "Figured. Thanks for saying it out loud." *(Trust +.)*
> *Shaken* — "That's about what I'd worked out. Doesn't change what we do." *(Nerve +1.)*
> *Breaking* — "Nine days. I keep — nine days is the thing I keep on." — pause — "Don't tell the lads I said that." *(Nerve +1, Trust ++. The only time he mentions the number. Never let anyone acknowledge it afterwards.)*

**Gone**
> *No reply. Anvil is a line squad on Intent, which means Anvil is holding whatever it was told to hold, indefinitely, until it can't.*

**If he dies:**
> **D. YARROW** — Sergeant, 2nd Squad — 2090–2141
> *Rotation was processed posthumously. His effects were forwarded to the address on file.*

**What you lose:** ground truth. There is no longer anyone whose reports can be taken at face value, which means every other character's filter must now be calibrated against nothing. Mechanically: the player's confidence in the whole board should drop, and the design should not compensate for it.

## LT. INES CALLOWAY — 4th Squad, "Harrow"
**Traits:** *Sharp* (+1 detail line) · *Certain* (always reports **CERTAIN**)

Twenty-six. Eleven sols in theatre, transferred from Staff at Pavonis where she was very good at a job that involved no one dying. Her observations are superb and her inferences are gospel. Every detail line she sends is *true*; the conclusion she draws from them in the same breath is frequently catastrophic.

She is the hardest read in the game, because she is not wrong about anything you can check. She is wrong about what it means.

**Voice:** Precise, staff-trained, reasons aloud from evidence to conclusion without pausing — which is exactly what makes her convincing. Uses "which means" as a hinge. Has never once written *I'd say*.

**Thread — reveals across sols 4, 9, 16:**
> *Sol 4.* Her file: Staff, Pavonis, exemplary. Zero combat commands.
> *Sol 9.* She has not lost anyone. She mentions this in a report, as a data point, in support of an argument.
> *Sol 16.* She does not know that this is luck. She has built a theory of herself out of eleven days of statistical noise, and she is about to test it.

**Design note — the timing relationship.** **LEVEL** on Calloway is catastrophic in Act I and transformative in Act III. It is the only character in the game where the *same button at the same Nerve state* has an inverted outcome depending on where the campaign is. The pivot is her first loss. Before it, telling her the truth destroys a structure holding her up. After it, it's the only thing that will.

### Conversation

**STEADY**
> *Steady* — "I'm not concerned, sir." *(No change. She isn't. That's the problem.)*
> *Shaken* — "Understood. I have it in hand." *(No change. Certain applies to her self-assessment too.)*
> *Breaking* — "I have it in hand." *(No change. She will say this at every state until she stops transmitting. The player cannot steady someone who will not report being unsteady.)*

**PRESS**
> *Steady* — "Already moving. It was the correct call." *(No change. She was going anyway.)*
> *Shaken* — "Moving. Though I'd note the ground doesn't support the assessment." *(Nerve −1.)*
> *Breaking* — "Moving." *(Nerve → Gone.)*

**LEVEL — before her first loss**
> *Steady* — "With respect, that isn't what the returns show." *(Nerve −1, Trust −. You have contradicted her in the only register she trusts.)*
> *Shaken* — "Then one of us has bad information, and I'm reading mine directly." *(Nerve −1.)*
> *Breaking* — "No. No, I've been *right*, I've been right the whole —" *(Nerve → Gone. The first sentence she has ever failed to finish.)*

**LEVEL — after her first loss**
> *Steady* — "...All right. Say the rest of it." *(Trust +.)*
> *Shaken* — "I'd like to hear it from you and not from the returns. Go on." *(Nerve +1, Trust +.)*
> *Breaking* — "I don't know what I'm looking at any more. Tell me what to do and I'll do it." — pause — "That's not me asking you to be certain. I know better now." *(Nerve +1, Trust ++.)*

**Gone**
> *No reply. Harrow is executing the last order it received with total confidence, which is the most dangerous thing on the board.*

**If she dies:**
> **I. CALLOWAY** — Lieutenant, 4th Squad — 2115–2141
> *Every observation in her final report was accurate.*

**What you lose:** the fourth axis. Calloway is the only source who *interprets*, and even wrong interpretation is a data point — a player who has learned her can invert her. Her replacement reports facts without theory, which is quieter and much less useful than it sounds.

# II. THE BENCH

Twenty-two names. They exist as vitals, casualties, promotions, and the occasional interjection under a squad leader's report. Nobody gets an arc. That's the design.

**1st, Wolfhound:** Cpl. N. Kolba · Rfn. Tsai · Rfn. Deyn · Rfn. Aduba · Rfn. Sarno
**2nd, Anvil:** Cpl. Tam Rusk · Rfn. Merrick · Rfn. Sowa · Rfn. Tobin · Rfn. Ives
**3rd, Kestrel:** Cpl. S. Nayar · Rfn. Quist · Rfn. Bel · Rfn. Ahn
**4th, Harrow:** Cpl. E. Dray · Rfn. Fen · Rfn. Okwe · Rfn. Sant · Rfn. Larke

**Death card format — no exceptions, no elaboration:**
```
        R. TSAI
     Rifleman, 1st Squad
        2119 — 2141
```
No cause. No last words. No mission context. The name, the squad, the two years. If the player wants to know what happened they can scroll up and find the report, and the report may not mention it.

### Two who matter

**CPL. NILS KOLBA — 1st Squad.** The promotion. When a leader dies, Kolba is usually who steps up, and his traits are *Green* (−1 detail, no recommendation) plus one hidden roll. He is competent and terrified and writes reports like a man filling in a form. Most players' first experience of the blindness that follows a death is Kolba's first report, which contains almost nothing.

**RFN. SENA ADUBA — 1st Squad.** Nineteen, on paper. Seventeen, actually. Lied at the recruiting office at Ascraeus because the Deep Cities don't take you and the Compact doesn't check. Her thread surfaces only if she survives to sol 12, and only in a single line under someone else's report:

> *[ADUBA, 0655] Sergeant, if I put my real age in, do they send me home or do they charge me?*

Nobody answers her. The report continues.

# III. IN THE ROOM

## WARRANT OFFICER TEODORA SALK — Ops, Bastion
The only person you speak to face to face. No traits, no filter, no Nerve — she is the one honest channel in the game, and the fiction has to earn that by making her honest in ways the player doesn't enjoy.

She runs the boards, pulls the personnel files, times the satellite windows, and disagrees with you. She is thirty-eight, on her second Mars tour, and does not think the war is winnable in the sense the Compact means.

**Function:** relief from the report format. Salk speaks in ordinary conversation, at length, in the room, with no latency. After four hours of clipped four-line transmissions this should feel almost obscene.

**Voice:** Dry, procedural, and then abruptly not. Delivers bad news by starting with the paperwork.

### Her arc, in five beats

> **Sol 1** — "Kestrel's your eyes and she's wrong about everything. You'll want to know by how much. I'd start a tally."

> **Sol 6** — "Balogun's put in for transfer twice. Both refused, both by the same signature." *(pause)* "I'm not telling you what to do with that. I'm telling you it's in the file and you're the only one who reads the file."

> **Sol 12** — "You've queried Division three times this week. I'd stop." *(pause)* "Not because they'll punish you. Because they've started answering, and the answers have got better, and nothing on the ground has changed."

> **Sol 17** — "I pulled the manifest for the convoy." *(long pause)* "It isn't a relief column. There's no water plant in it. There's sixty tonnes of shaped charge and a survey team, and the routing has it at the Cistern, not at Bastion." *(pause)* "I want you to tell me I've read it wrong."

> **Sol 21, if the player complies** — "Log it however you want. I'll countersign it." *(pause)* "That's not absolution. That's just what a warrant officer does."

> **Sol 21, if the player refuses** — "Right." *(pause)* "You know they'll have you for this." *(pause)* "Give me the order in writing and I'll transmit it. You're not doing that part alone."

## "DIVISION"
Not a person. A signature block. Every FRAGO arrives from DIVISION and the game never puts a face on it until sol 14.

**Voice:** Institutional. Passive voice for anything unpleasant. Active voice for anything that flatters. Never says *I*.

> *"It has been decided that the Halloran Cut is to be held through the night. Withdrawal is not authorised. Adequate support will be provided."*

## COL. HADDE RENFREW — Compact Operations, Pavonis
Revealed sol 14. The signature behind Division, and — the detail that should turn the player's stomach — the same signature that refused Balogun's transfers, twice, for reasons of unit cohesion.

He is not a villain and must never be written as one. He is a competent administrator executing a water allocation on a ninety-year timescale, and he has correctly calculated that four hundred thousand people in the Deep Cities is a smaller number than the population of a habitable Mars in 2400. He will say this, calmly, once, and it will be the most frightening thing in the game because it is arithmetic.

> **Sol 14, first direct contact.** "You've been querying. That's fine — it's in the manual, it's why the channel exists." *(pause)* "But I'd like you to be clear that when you ask me whether an order is necessary, you're asking me to do a sum I have already done, and to show you the working because you'd like to feel better about it. I don't mind. I'd just like us both to know what's happening."

> **Sol 18, if queried on the convoy.** "The aquifer is a hundred and ten billion litres. Spent on the Deep Cities it buys four hundred thousand people about sixty years. Spent on atmosphere it buys everyone who comes after them a planet." *(pause)* "You're going to tell me those people are real and the others aren't. You're right. That's the whole of the disagreement, and I've heard it, and my answer is that I'm paid to count the ones who aren't here yet."

> **Sol 21, if refused.** "Noted. Relief is en route and your company will be withdrawn." *(pause)* "For what it's worth, Major — I don't think you're wrong. I think you're early, and I think being early is the same as being wrong when there's a convoy on the road."

## STEWARD ILSABET ROQUE — Deep Cities
Heard only through intercepts until Act III. The elected water-warden of Pavonis, which is a civil office, not a military one — she is a hydrologist who ended up commanding because the person who should have been doing it died.

She is reasonable, tired, and entirely correct about what the Compact is going to do, and the player will hear her being correct for two acts before they believe her.

**Intercept voice:** mid-conversation, administrative, never rhetorical. She is usually heard *managing* something rather than fighting.

> `—— no, leave the heaters running. Let them look. ——`
> `—— tell Ascraeus we can hold the ration at four litres through the season but not past it ——`
> `—— they're not going to hold the Cut, they're going to hold it until something else is ready, that's a different —— [cut]`
> `—— I'd rather talk to their commander than their colonel. Is there a way to do that. ——`

**Act III direct contact** — available only if the player has kept Company Trust high and deployed a relay beacon at Cold Harbour:

> "This is Roque. I'm told you're the one who's been listening." *(pause)* "I'm not going to ask you to surrender and you're not going to ask me. I want to know whether you've read the convoy manifest, and I want you to know that if you have, and you sit on it, there are four hundred thousand people who will find out eventually who you were."

# IV. RULES FOR ALL FUTURE CHARACTER TEXT

1. **Nobody is eloquent about death.** When a soldier loses someone the line is short, practical, and about something else. *"Kolba's hurt, walking."* Grief is in what the report doesn't get to.
2. **Never write the absence.** Balogun's reports must read as complete documents. If the player can detect an omission from the prose alone, the trait has failed — telemetry is the only place a gap is allowed to show.
3. **Compression, not volume.** Steady Rivas writes forty words. Breaking Rivas writes eleven. Nobody shouts on the net.
4. **The enemy is never contemptible.** Every Roque intercept is her doing her job well.
5. **Hesketh is never wrong on the facts.** The moment he lies, the player is off the hook, and the player must never be off the hook.
6. **No one ever confirms ground truth.** Not Salk, not a debrief, not an ending. Salk can suspect. Salk cannot know.
