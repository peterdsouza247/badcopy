# BAD COPY — SETTING BIBLE

---

## 1. WHY MARS IS BETTER

Mars has no global magnetosphere and effectively no ionosphere. There is no skywave propagation — radio cannot bounce over the horizon the way it does on Earth. And because Mars is small, the horizon is *close*: about 3.4 km at standing height, against 5 km on Earth.

So military communication on Mars is line-of-sight or it is nothing. Everything else runs through infrastructure: a relay tower network and a thin satellite constellation, both of which can be shot, jammed, or simply not built yet.

**The consequence, which is the whole game:**

> A canyon is a place where your people stop existing.

DARK is no longer a timer. It is a decision the player makes when they send a squad into terrain. You are not waiting out an orbit — you chose this, for a reason that seemed good, and now Kestrel has been gone eleven turns.

## 2. WHERE

**Noctis Labyrinthus** — the Labyrinth of Night. A real feature: a 1,200 km maze of intersecting canyons and collapsed grabens where Tharsis meets Valles Marineris. Walls up to 5 km. Floors that branch, rejoin, and dead-end.

It is, by geography, a permanent communications shadow. It is also the best cover on the planet, which is why everyone fights there.

**Year:** 2141. Terraforming is ninety years in and roughly a third finished. Atmosphere is thickening — thin cloud, real weather, dust that now has enough air to move properly. Pressure is survivable for minutes, not hours. Nobody breathes outside.

### Key locations

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Bastion** | Your command post. Buried ops centre at the head of the Labyrinth. You never leave it. |
| **The Cistern** | The Noctis aquifer works. Subsurface ice-melt and pumping plant. The reason for the war. |
| **The Chain** | The relay tower line running the length of the Labyrinth. Fragile, contested, and the only reason you hear anything at all. |
| **Halloran Cut** | A side canyon with the best crest on the Chain. Changes hands repeatedly. |
| **Pilgrim's Stair** | Switchback route from the floor to the Shelf. The only vehicle path. |
| **Cold Harbour** | A dead pumping station, half-buried. Where things go to be hidden. |
| **The Sink** | Deepest graben in the network. Zero contact, always. Nobody has ever ordered a squad in there and heard from them the same day. |

## 3. WHO IS FIGHTING, AND WHY IT'S NOT SIMPLE

**The Tharsis Compact** — Earth-chartered administration of Mars. Your employer. Terraforming is their mandate and their business model; the water in the Noctis aquifer is the single largest unallocated reserve on the planet, and it is earmarked for atmospheric processing.

**The Deep Cities** — Pavonis, Ascraeus and Arsia. Buried arcologies under the Tharsis volcanoes, populated by second- and third-generation Martians who have never been to Earth and never will. Roughly four hundred thousand people. Their water comes from the Noctis aquifer.

The Compact wants to spend the water on a planet that doesn't exist yet. The Deep Cities want to drink it.

**Neither side is written as wrong.** Terraforming genuinely is the long survival of Mars; the Compact is not cackling. The Deep Cities are also not innocents — they have been cutting the Chain and killing engineers for two years. The player should be able to construct a defensible case for either side, and the company will slowly construct one for the other side whether the player likes it or not.

This is what gives **Company Trust** teeth. Your soldiers are not losing faith in *you*. They are losing faith in the reason.

## 4. THE COMMS MODEL

Four states. Shown as one word at the top of the FEED, always.

| State | Latency | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **CLEAR** | 10–40 sec | Direct LOS or healthy relay |
| **RELAYED** | 2–8 min | Signal is hopping the Chain. Degrades with each hop. |
| **THIN** | 8–25 min | Satellite pass only. Windows are short and scheduled. |
| **DARK** | ∞ | No path exists. Nothing in, nothing out. |

**What the player controls:**

- **Where they send people.** Every board node has a published comms state. The route to the objective and the route that keeps you in contact are almost never the same route.
- **Relay beacons.** Squads carry two each. Deploying one converts a DARK node to RELAYED for the rest of the mission — and puts a permanent, visible emitter on the map that the enemy can see just as clearly as you can. The single best risk decision in the game.
- **The Chain itself.** Towers can be taken, held, lost. Losing a tower doesn't just cost you a node; it degrades everything downstream of it. A player who has been sloppy about the Chain will discover it in Act III, all at once.

**What the player doesn't control:**

- **Dust.** Martian dust is triboelectrically charged; a storm fills the air with static and shorts the spectrum. Local storms cost hours. The global dust season arrives in Act III and it is not survivable as an information problem — it is the reason Act III is designed to be played half-blind.
- **Terrain.** The Sink is DARK forever. That's not a puzzle to solve.

## 5. WHAT SUITS CHANGE

Environment as a second clock, and it's a hard one.

- **Breach.** A suit breach is minutes, not hours. Every casualty report is time-critical in a way that makes "hold position" a real cruelty.
- **Cold.** Night is −80°C on the floor. A squad that can't get to shelter degrades whether or not anyone shoots at them.
- **0.38 g.** Movement is fast and stopping is slow. Every squad moves further than the player expects, which is how orders get outrun by events.
- **Dust on optics.** Thermal is the primary sensor and dust degrades it. This is why heat-decoys work, why counts are always wrong, and why *"too cold, too even"* is a line four different people can report and only one can interpret.
- **Water is ammunition.** Suit recycling is imperfect. A squad out of contact for a long patrol is also a squad running down its reclaim. Nobody has to say this out loud; it just makes the Sink terrifying.
